    6. Investigations
    NICE: CT not routinely recommended after first seizure if examination normal. Indications for urgent CT: focal neurology, raised ICP, head injury, immunocompromised, not returned to baseline.
    MRI brain (preferred over CT) should be organised within 6 weeks of diagnosis, and is indicated in ALL children UNLESS: (1) Idiopathic generalised epilepsy, OR (2) SeLECTS with complete seizure control on first-line medication.
    ⚠️ EEG is NOT routinely indicated for a FIRST generalised tonic-clonic seizure

    EEG indications — Partners in Paediatrics 2025-28 guideline

    ✅ EEG Indicated

    • To characterise electroclinical syndrome AFTER clinical diagnosis of epilepsy
    • After unprovoked status epilepticus
    • Suspicion of non-convulsive status in a child with known epilepsy
    • Acquired regression of speech/language

    ❌ EEG NOT Indicated

    • Non-epileptic episodes (syncope, reflex anoxic seizures, breath-holding) — ECG more appropriate
    • Seizures in context of febrile infection
    • FIRST generalised tonic-clonic seizures
    • To monitor well-controlled epilepsy
    • Before stopping treatment
    Note to clinician: EEG should NOT be performed routinely in ED as an emergency — arrange outpatient EEG via paediatric neurology only where indicated above.

    Indicated if:

    • Epilepsy onset <2 years with no cause found on imaging
    • Epilepsy in a child with intellectual disability / autism / dysmorphic features / cognitive decline, with no cause found on imaging

    9. Management & Disposition
    ⚠️ Phenytoin (and other sodium-channel blockers — lamotrigine, carbamazepine) is CONTRAINDICATED in suspected Dravet syndrome (e.g. febrile status epilepticus <12 months of age) — use sodium valproate/levetiracetam instead and refer urgently to tertiary neurology.

    Dextrose 10% 2ml/kg if hypoglycaemic

    Ceftriaxone 80mg/kg (max 4g) if bacterial meningitis suspected

    Advise: call 999 if seizure lasts >5 minutes, another seizure occurs, child is not waking up, or new focal features develop.
